This example illustrates how propagation exposure may vary across time and directed network segments within the same constrained system.

The labels represent movement segments and nodes within the network structure. For example, `Segment A1→X` represents agents moving toward node `X` along a directed network path.

Higher values indicate combinations of local capacity pressure and downstream coupling exposure associated with elevated propagation exposure.

The figure illustrates several qualitative behaviors implied by the structural formulation of Model v2:

- propagation exposure depends on both timing and network position 
- structurally central regions may exhibit elevated exposure
- similar local conditions may produce different propagation potential depending on downstream coupling opportunities
- propagation exposure may vary dynamically over time even within the same network structure

This example illustrates how structurally different scenarios may generate different levels of system-level propagation impact.

The purpose is to demonstrate that propagation impact is not determined by disturbance magnitude alone. Timing, clustering, structural position, and downstream coupling opportunities may cause smaller or moderate disturbances to generate larger system-level effects than a single isolated disruption.

The examples are hypothetical and intended to illustrate the type of comparative structural analysis supported by the model formulation.

Potential operational interpretations and analytical uses include:

- comparison of how different timing–location–disturbance combinations generate different levels of system-level impact
- analysis of how clustered disturbances may produce disproportionate propagation effects

The comparison may be performed across different time windows, operating conditions, or subsystem boundaries depending on the analytical context.
