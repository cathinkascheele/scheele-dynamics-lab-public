# Analytical Checklist

For analyses that support a conclusion, interpretation, or decision, the following points should be considered and, where relevant, documented.

The checklist is intended both as:
- a guide during analysis, and
- a record of the analytical reasoning behind the final result.

Not every item will be equally relevant to every analysis. If an item is
not applicable, this should be stated explicitly.

The level of documentation should be proportional to the scope, complexity,
and decision relevance of the analysis.

## 1. Problem
- What question am I actually answering?
- What decision or understanding should the analysis support?

## 2. Scope
- What level of analytical depth is required for the question or decision?
- What is deliberately outside the scope?
- What additional analysis could materially change the conclusion?

## 3. Representation
- What does one observation represent?
- What system properties am I representing?
- What information is lost through aggregation?
- What is the reference state / baseline?

## 4. Method
- What quantities do I calculate?
- Why do these quantities answer the question?
- How are they calculated?
- What transformations/normalisations are applied?

## 5. Assumptions
- What must be true for the calculations and comparisons to be meaningful?

## 6. Epistemic level
- Descriptive?
- Diagnostic?
- Predictive?
- Causal?

## 7. Validation
- Are the data valid for this purpose?
- Are the calculations correct?
- Are conclusions robust to reasonable analytical choices?

## 8. Limitations
- What can the analysis not establish?

## 9. Selection / decision relevance
- What gets surfaced?
- What gets ignored?
- Why?
- Are thresholds heuristic or empirically justified?

## 10. Further work
- What remains unresolved?
